## 1. Hunt in Barbizon (c. 1852-1855)

**Arrival and departure. Not settled.** No reachable source gives a day or
month. What they say:
- Met catalogue entry (American Wing, author not named, cat. no. 39):
  Hunt saw Millet's peasant scenes at the 1850 Salon and « soon moved to
  Barbizon, outside Paris, to work near Millet »; he took *The Sower* back
  to Boston in 1855. Tier 2.
- Smithsonian American Art Museum biography (citing Bermingham, *American
  Art in the Barbizon Mood*, 1975): « most of the next two years with
  Millet at Barbizon », after the Sower at the 1851 Salon. Tier 2.
- Brockett, *Antiques & Fine Art* (citing Webster 1991): « Hunt studied
  with Millet in 1853 », « spending two years in Barbizon ». Tier 2.
- Met 08.88 *Girl at the Fountain*: « registered as a picture begun in
  1852 », « completion by 1854 », sister Jane as model, pose from a
  coachman watering horses. Tier 1 for the painting, which places Hunt
  painting figure subjects in 1852-54 but does not say where.
- en.wikipedia: « two years with Millet at Barbizon », married Louisa
  Dumaresq Perkins in Paris in 1855 shortly before returning. Tier 3.
- Working bracket: Barbizon from 1852 or 1853 to 1855 (marriage in Paris,
  return to America the same year). The exact months need Knowlton 1899
  chapter II and Angell 1881: `[needs-local]`.

**Lodging.** Settled earlier: first a room at Jean Gatelier's (Knowlton
p. 62), then « a comfortable home » (Shannon 1923). The 40 Grande Rue
tradition stays a lead pending AD77 (task ce8efd66). Nothing new tonight.

**What he painted there. Lead only.** *Girl at the Fountain* (Met 08.88,
1852-54, begun 1852) is the one securely dated work of the period reached
tonight; the Met entry ties its subject to Millet's influence and its
handling to Couture, not to a Barbizon site. The Boston memorial
exhibition of 1879 registered it as begun in 1852. No reachable source
lists Barbizon-subject canvases with titles and dates. Not found: a list
of works painted at Barbizon.

**Daily relationship with Millet.**
- Rolland, *Millet* (1902, Wikisource): Hunt « bought the Woman shearing
  Sheep and The Shepherd », and Millet said he « was the best and most
  intimate friend he ever had ». Tier 2 (early secondary, in English, no
  footnote for the Millet quotation; the likely origin is Wheelwright,
  *Atlantic Monthly*, Sept. 1876, not reached).
- Hurll, *Jean François Millet* (1900, Gutenberg): table of events, Salon
  of 1853, *The Sheep Shearer* and *The Shepherd* « bought by William
  Morris Hunt »; list of « Friends at Barbizon » includes « William
  Morris Hunt, American painter »; Boston appreciation « owing to the
  enthusiasm of William Morris Hunt ». Tier 2.
- Two independent Tier 2 sources agree on the two 1853 Salon purchases:
  **confirmed** as a claim of the early literature; the MFA accession
  numbers for these two could not be checked tonight (MFA blocked).
- Millet's own letters (Sensier 1881, Moreau-Nélaton 1921) were not
  reachable: `[needs-local: gallica Sensier 1881 « Hunt »; Moreau-Nélaton
  1921 t. II « Hunt »]`. Not found: any letter text.

## 2. Support for Millet and the others

## 3. Hunt as promoter in America

- Boston collectors: Hurll 1900 credits Boston's early appreciation of
  Millet to « the enthusiasm of William Morris Hunt » and « Mr. Quincy
  Shaw and Mr. Brimmer ». Tier 2. Frick Center for the History of
  Collecting: Brimmer (1829-1896) owned works by « J. F. Millet » and
  « W. M. Hunt » (*Art Treasures of America*), no dates. Tier 1 directory,
  thin. Jamaica Plain Historical Society (1992): Shaw « began collecting
  French painting in 1860 », « did not purchase from Millet until a third
  trip to Paris in 1874 », later « 54 of Millet's paintings, pastels and
  etchings ». Tier 3, no Hunt mentioned. Thomas Gold Appleton: not found
  in any reachable source tonight.
- Allston Club 1866: Met catalogue entry, Hunt helped found it,
  « organized in opposition to the more traditional strictures of the
  Boston Athenaeum »; Brockett (citing Webster): « Over half of the works
  shown at their first exhibition were by French artists ». Two Tier 2:
  **confirmed**. Courbet's *The Quarry* was « sold ... to the Allston Club
  in Boston in 1866 » (en.wikipedia citing MFA record, Tseng 2008, Chu
  1997); the New Criterion (Alspaugh 2013) says Hunt « was instrumental
  in bringing The Quarry to Boston ». Lead for Hunt's personal role.
- Teaching: classes began winter 1868, students women « primarily from
  affluent backgrounds », Knowlton « took over the teaching from Hunt » in
  1871 (Brockett, citing Hirshler 2001). en.wikipedia: at the request of
  Helen M. Knowlton, criticised by peers. Lead (one Tier 2 + Tier 3).
- Priority claim (« one of the first Americans to collect and promote the
  Barbizon School »). What the reachable literature supports: Hunt is
  consistently named as the *channel* through which Millet reached Boston
  (Hurll 1900, SAAM/Bermingham 1975, Brockett/Webster). Edward
  Wheelwright, Bostonian, studied with Millet at Barbizon (1855-56 per the
  standard dating, not verified tonight) and wrote the *Atlantic Monthly*
  sketch of Sept. 1876 (Hofstadter, New Criterion 1984). Hunt's portrait
  of Wheelwright is dated 1857. Boston Athenaeum 1850s French exhibitions:
  not found tonight. Scholarly statements on priority (Bermingham 1975,
  Hoppin, Webster 1991, Murphy 1984): not reached. The safe wording is
  « the first American painter to live beside Millet and bring his work to
  Boston », not « the first American to collect Barbizon painting ».
  Status: open.

## 5. Last years and death

**Albany murals.** Commission 1878 for the Assembly Chamber of the new
State Capitol, two murals, *The Flight of Night* and *The Discoverer*
(Met catalogue entry; empirestateplaza.ny.gov names both titles and
Hunt). Robinson: 16 x 45 feet each, painted over the winter, dedicated
6 January 1879 (Tier 3 for these details). Painted on the stone itself;
dampness in the stone caused deterioration (Brockett/Webster; Oaklander
« inherent vice », « suffering soon after their completion »). Covered:
NY State page gives « papier-mâché drop ceiling, 1889-1911 »; Robinson
says covered in 1888; en.wikipedia says the panels disintegrated. Hunt
did not live to see them covered. Two Tier 2 for commission and
deterioration: **confirmed**; the covering date is a lead (1888 vs
1889). An assistant, John G. Carter, « committed suicide a few months
after the mural was completed » (Oaklander, Tier 3, unverified).

**Health and marriage.** Robinson: a condition « that weakened his
muscles », exhaustion after Albany; separated from his wife Louisa Perkins
in 1874. en.wikipedia: « periods of depression » [citation needed]. Met
catalogue: the murals « contributed to the artist's physical undoing ».
Lead.

**Death: date and place.** 8 September 1879, Appledore Island, Isles of
Shoals, body in a small pond near the centre of the island (Robinson;
Find a Grave; Vose). Met: « drowned ... in September 1879, while visiting
friends at Appledore ». Find a Grave records the death in Kittery, York
County, Maine (Appledore lies in Kittery) and burial at Prospect Hill
Cemetery, Brattleboro, Vermont, section 1-200. Date confirmed by
convergence (Met, Vose, Find a Grave, Wikipedia citing NYT 9 Sept 1879);
the civil record itself not seen.

**Cause, view by view (recorded, not concluded).**
1. *New York Times*, 9 Sept 1879, « Death of William Morris Hunt »:
   reported, per Robinson and en.wikipedia, that Hunt had taken his own
   life; Wikipedia's phrase is « apparently a suicide ». Not read.
   `[needs-local: NYT 9 Sept 1879; Chronicling America 8-15 Sept 1879
   « Hunt » « Appledore »]`.
2. Celia Thaxter, letters (1895 ed., Fields and Lamb): « I found him »;
   « It was mockery. He had been dead for hours »; she noted the back of
   his coat was dry (Robinson's reading: fell forward). Quoted via
   Robinson, Tier 3 relay of a Tier 2 text. `[needs-local: Letters of
   Celia Thaxter, 1895, letters of Sept. 1879, HathiTrust
   mdp.39015013757813]`.
3. The examining physician (unnamed, per Robinson): likely a dizzy spell
   while walking in the rain with an umbrella; the umbrella snapped; a
   piece was found across the pool. Accident. Source not identified.
4. MFA memorial exhibition catalogue, 1879: « A suspicion seems to have
   existed at the time that his death was a voluntary one », arguing
   that his disability « diminish[ed] his power of resistance to
   accident ». Via Robinson. Open, leaning accident.
5. Robinson (seacoastnh, 2011): the cause « was later amended to
   accidental drowning », the debate continues; Hunt disappointed that
   expected further commissions did not come in 1879; a rumour that the
   state had not paid him, denied by the Capitol's Stuart Lehman.
6. Met catalogue entry, SAAM, Vose, Hurll: « drowned », no cause given.
7. AAA blog (2010): « committed suicide in 1879 ». Brockett: « reportedly
   suicide ». eclecticlight: « thought to have been suicide ».
   en.wikipedia: the murals' decay « is believed to have led to his
   depression and presumed suicide », citing an archived Pierce
   Galleries dealer page for the scholars' view.
8. Who first suggested suicide: on tonight's evidence the earliest
   suicide statement is the NYT report of 9 Sept 1879 (one day after the
   death); who within the household or press originated it is not found.
   Who links the fire losses as motive: nobody reachable tonight; the
   motive discussed is the Albany murals (decay, pay, no new
   commissions), plus health and the 1874 separation. Knowlton 1899,
   Hoppin and Webster 1991 on the death: not reached.
