ContentSearch, ALTO, IIIF). Nothing here has been read on the page image
unless stated; everything is a lead.

## Villa Bernard
- L'Informateur de Fontainebleau, 19 July 1932, p. 8, classified
  advertisement (ALTO OCR): "MAISON DE REPOS POUR ENFANTS. Villa Bernard,
  Barbizon ; téléph. 88." https://gallica.bnf.fr/ark:/12148/bpt6k51806898/f8
  Suggests the villa was a children's rest home in 1932. Read on the image
  and look for other years before use.

## Coz Ker (villa)
- SRU, all types, exact phrase "Coz Ker" AND Barbizon: 3 hits (La Dépêche de
  Brest 1941, Le Saint-Hubert 1909, Karampournis 1990). Only the last is
  relevant so far.
- Roger Karampournis, *Si Barbizon m'était conté...*, 1990
  (bpt6k3366111z), in copyright: ALTO and page images are blocked, only
  ContentSearch snippets are available.
  - View 136 (snippet): Marguerite, daughter of Alfred Sensier, married
    Théodore Duhamel, later mayor of the XIe arrondissement of Paris
    (1902-1904), who "construisit par la suite une belle maison dans la
    Grande rue de Barbizon, la Villa Marguerite encore visible de nos jours".
  - View 137 (snippet, apparently a footnote): "Actuellement villa Coz Ker
    34, Grande rue".
  - Reading: the footnote probably identifies the Villa Marguerite (Duhamel,
    Sensier's son-in-law) as today's villa Coz Ker, 34 Grande Rue. The same
    page also discusses a villa begun around 1948 by Hatcher in Millet's
    garden, so the footnote could attach to that instead; Millet's garden is
    on the odd side of the Grande Rue, which makes the Villa Marguerite the
    likelier match. Needs the physical book (or the Barbizon library copy)
    before any public use.
