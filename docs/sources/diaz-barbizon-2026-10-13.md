## Answers by question

**Q1. Where did Diaz live or stay?** Two Diaz places, over time:
1. **Corner of Grande Rue / route de Macherin (Le Relais de Barbizon)**: his first house, per Gassies des Brûlies via Lemoyne de Forges (1971), the 1975 catalogue, Leprohon (1985), Karampournis (1990) and the Inventory (2019). All later secondary; no period source found yet. Dates unknown (from the late 1830s or 1840s).
2. **The house later called « maison de Diaz » (no. 28, Le Petit Manoir)**: period sources call it Diaz's studio or former home: « l'atelier de Diaz » (1899, 1909), « ancienne habitation de Diaz et de Céramano » (three 1892 sources, image-checked once), « maison de Diaz » (Figaro 1903, image), and in 1911 a « villa genre petit manoir [...] ancienne habitation du peintre Diaz » on the Grande Rue. Céramano moved into it in 1871. So Diaz had left it by 1871.
3. **No. 11 (Auberge Diaz)**: no evidence Diaz lived there. See Q6.
Independent period sources: 6 for the Diaz studio/house that Céramano and Comble later occupied (3 on the image); 0 for the Macherin corner.
Caution: no period source in hand gives the street number. The match with no. 28 rests on the « petit manoir » wording (1911), the plaque (1903) and the faux-Gothic description (1971).

**Q2. Did he own property?** Probably yes (Karampournis and Leprohon say he bought the Macherin farm and later ceded it to Taine), but no deed, sale notice or cadastre entry found. For the studio-house: we know who came after him (Céramano from 1871; Paul Comble, buyer by 1894), not whether Diaz owned it or how it passed. Period sources: 0 on Diaz's ownership.

**Q3. First visit and frequency.** Diaz was painting at Apremont by the Salon of 1837 (Herbet 1903, OCR); secondary chronologies give 1836 (Lyon 2002; Inventory 2019). Frequent summer stays in the 1840s-1850s (Ganne's inn, Gassies). In his last years he came « bien rarement » (Gassies 1907). Period sources: 2 (Herbet, Gassies), both OCR. « 1837 to his death » is not supported.

**Q4. Taine.** Taine was at Barbizon with his mother on 15 June 1867 (his letter, image-checked) and at a gathering there in Oct. 1868 (press, snippet); Billy says he was still there in 1891. That he lived in Diaz's former house at the Macherin corner rests only on Lemoyne de Forges, the 1975 catalogue and Karampournis (secondary). Period sources: 2 for Taine at Barbizon; 0 for the house.

**Q5. Cuvelier and Comble at no. 28.** Comble: Paul Comble painted the house in 1892 (then empty), had bought it by 1894, rebuilt the studio by 1899 (image-checked), and showed visitors round it in 1903. Jean-Baptiste Comble became first mayor in Jan. 1904. Whether Paul and Jean-Baptiste are the same man is unproven. **Cuvelier: nothing found** linking Eugène Cuvelier to this house. Period sources: 4 for Comble (2 on the image); 0 for Cuvelier.

**Q6. Auberge Diaz.** A 20th-century business named after the painter: attested 1948-1963 (Gallica, Guide Bleu), placed « rue du Bas-Bréau », « à l'entrée de la forêt » (1949), and claiming in 1954-55 to be in « l'ancien atelier Diaz de la Peña ». Business sold in March 1962. Nothing links it to Émile Diaz. Sources: 4+ (all snippets).

**Q7. Renoir anecdote.** First printed by Meier-Graefe (German 1911, French 1912, p.16): met Diaz in 1862 « à Chailly dans le bois de Fontainebleau », credit at the colour merchant. Renoir's own version (Vollard 1918-1920): the fight, the wooden leg, « pourquoi diable peignez-vous si noir », in the « forêt de Fontainebleau », with no place named. Jean Renoir (1962) is a later retelling. No source found that says « Barbizon »; Meier-Graefe's « Chailly » (the commune that then included Barbizon) is the nearest. Sources: 2 period (both OCR).

**Q8. Picasso at the Hôtel Bellevue.** Not supported. The hotel is attested at Barbizon only from 1931 to 1954; no press, catalogue or book found placing Picasso, Olga or Paulo at Barbizon. Sources agree on a rented villa in Fontainebleau, July to September 1921. Period sources: 0.

## Conflicts

- **Website vs sources:** « at no. 28 from 1837 until his death » conflicts with Gassies (rare visits late in life), Céramano in the studio from 1871, and Diaz's death at Menton (1876).
- **Mairie map / plaque vs Gassies des Brûlies:** the plaque house (no. 28) vs the Macherin corner (Le Relais). The sources can both be right if Diaz used both places at different times; none of them dates either one.
- **Auberge Diaz location:** mairie map (no. 11, now La Folie Barbizon) vs Karampournis (became the Hostellerie de la Dague). Check whether La Dague was at no. 11.
- **1836 vs 1837** for Diaz's first stay.
- **Paul Comble vs Jean-Baptiste Comble:** the mairie map names J.-B. Comble (first mayor); the press names the painter Paul Comble as the buyer.
- **Cuvelier:** the mairie map has him at no. 28; no source found.
