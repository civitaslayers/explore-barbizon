- The 1895 house was small; the present large building existed by c.1900 (Luigi, postcards), so it was built or enlarged 1896-1900 (Duhamel or early Schaller). **LEAD**
- Parcels today: AK 313 (1,828 m²), 315 (88 m²), 325 (3,816 m², 38 bis). Total ~5,700 m² vs 2,219 m² in 1895: the hotel absorbed neighbouring land. **CONFIRMED (cadastre)**
- The mairie village map date « 1872 » for the hotel: no support; no Charmettes before Sept 1896. **CONFIRMED absent**
- 1915: 25 convalescent soldiers housed (Rép. S.-et-M. 13 May 1915). **CONFIRMED**

### Bodmer and Hunt at no. 40 (OPEN)
- Local tradition (Cercle des Amis de Barbizon, blog « Barbizon, le guide », 2015; German Wikipedia; a translated panel in the building): Hunt's house where he kept his horses; Bodmer bought it in 1856 for 4,000 F; Rodolphe Bodmer left « une dette record d'apéritifs » at the Schallers'. Unsourced. **LEAD**
- Bodmer's residence 1856-1886 was NOT the Landerer house: census order (1872/1876/1886) places him ~6 households after Rousseau's house (Chevillard) and 15-20 before Landerer; the 1887 sale notice gives him neighbours Caron (E, = Lansraux/Caron household in census) and Favard (W and behind), and « tient par devant, au midi, à la rue » (north side). Best estimate: north side, roughly today's 50-64. **CONFIRMED (two sources agree)**
- Bodmer's 1867 advert (Moniteur universel 19 Sept 1867, bpt6k4440583v/f4): walled Grande Rue property with grange, **écurie**, planted courtyard, 3 gardens, ~2,200 m², « divisible en deux », 24,000 F. Matches the 1895 Landerer plot closely. Hypothesis: Hunt's property → Bodmer 1856 (second property, not his residence) → sold after 1867 → Landerer. **LEAD, decisive test = AD77 hypothèques/enregistrement**
- Hunt: first room rented from Jean Gatelier, rabbit-seller (Knowlton 1899 p.62); « made for himself a comfortable home » (Shannon 1923 p.37); no purchase recorded. Jean Gatelier's house is in the west part of the village (next to Babcock in 1872). **CONFIRMED**
- Bodmer: lived in Barbizon from c.1849 (press) / first census 1856; married Anna Maria Pfeiffer 1876 (census shows unmarried to 1872); sons Charles Henri (b. c.1855, photographer 1886-87), Rodolphe (b. c.1856 Chailly, painter, alive in Barbizon 1920), Henri Adolphe (b. c.1863). 1884 séparation de biens; 1885 seizure (5,000 F debt, sale stopped by Louis Renaud); house auctioned 29 July 1887, re-offered 23 Sept 1887, 8,000 F reserve (« maison de campagne avec atelier de peintre, corps de bâtiment séparé, cour et jardin »); buyer unknown. Absent 1891 census. Died Paris, 24 place Denfert-Rochereau, c.31 Oct 1893 (Le Briard 3 Nov 1893); buried Chailly. **CONFIRMED**
- Galerie contemporaine 1877 tome III no.72 « Karl Bodmer » (original sheet in the building): last page missing; full run at Bibliothèque Forney, PER H32 Rés Fol. **OPEN**

## 2. Occupation 1940-1944

- Hélène Schaller ran the hotel 1940-42 (Le Briard 19 Feb 1940, 10 July 1942; Rép. 17 Apr 1940). Hotel absent from press July 1942-1946. **CONFIRMED**
- Drue Tartière, The House Near Paris (1946) p.285-286: end of July 1944 « the Gestapo had arrested the proprietor of the Hôtel Les Charmettes and his wife ». Unnamed. **CONFIRMED (memoir)**
- Citroën family (Mme Citroën and child) sheltered at the Charmettes: told to Luigi by Gilles Schaller (Hélène's grandson), who heard it from a Citroën descendant. No documentary trace (Gallica, Yad Vashem, AJPN). **ORAL**
- Princess Elizabeth chose the Charmettes because the Bas-Bréau collaborated: **ORAL, not publishable**. Combat 18 May 1948: British Embassy chose Barbizon for painting and Resistance (six British parachutists hidden); Le Figaro: good food and easier to guard.
- Bas-Bréau: Rommel stayed often, Keitel visited, Gestapo chief Pat dined there (Tartière p.77-78, 247-249); proprietress Mme Buffo warned Tartière. Requisitioned 1946 by the préfecture for French officers (Rép. 6 Feb 1946). Anzo Fava owner 1941. No evidence of collaboration. **CONFIRMED (facts only)**
- Airmen: Lancaster shot down 5-6 July 1944; Lorne « Red » Frame (RCAF), Bill Watson, Don Steepe, Dannie Murphy hidden by Tartière and May de Gesnes (Tartière p.259-283). Barbizon liberated 23 Aug 1944; rue de Chailly renamed rue du 23-Août (Rép. 6 Dec 1944, André Billy). **CONFIRMED**
