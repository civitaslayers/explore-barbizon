## Les Charmettes (40 Grande Rue)

### Guests and residents
- Roger Martin du Gard (Nobel 1937) wrote his first novel *Devenir!* at Barbizon, Hôtel des Charmettes, 1908.
  - Sicard, *Roger Martin du Gard: les années d'apprentissage littéraire* (1976) p.33: "Barbizon, Hôtel des Charmettes, il rédige son roman de 'Fantoche', qu'il se décide à appeler Devenir"; chronology p.461: idea 27 Feb 1908, first draft 15 May to early July, Barbizon then Le Tertre.
  - BnF exhibition catalogue *Roger Martin du Gard* (Callu, Bléchet, Brunet, 1981) p.29: autograph scenario headed "FANTOCHE. Barbizon, 15 mai 1908" (BnF Mss, fonds RMG).
  - Brenner, *Martin du Gard* (1961) p.21: in 1908 he settles with his wife and daughter in an inn at Barbizon and writes Devenir! in one go; published Ollendorff that autumn at author's expense.
  - Conflict: Universalis says "à la fin de l'année"; the dated manuscript and Sicard's chronology give May to July 1908. Prefer spring/summer 1908.
- André Rouveyre (caricaturist, writer, friend of Apollinaire, Gide, Matisse) lived at the Charmettes in the early 1920s.
  - Letters headed "Les Charmettes, Barbizon": to Georg Brandes 30 Jan 1923 (Brandes, *La France et l'Italie*, 1952, p.395); to Gide 6 June 1923 (*Gide-Rouveyre correspondance*, ed. Claude Martin, 1967, p.62); 17 Oct 1924 (Australian Journal of French Studies 7, 1970, p.34).
  - Coquio, *André Rouveyre 1879-1962* (1995) p.31: chronology, settles at the hôtel des Charmettes de Barbizon (c.1919-1920 entry).
  - Billy, *Les beaux jours de Barbizon* p.145: Billy found Rouveyre installed at the Charmettes; they had spent summer 1920 together at the Hôtel des Voyageurs. Billy, *Le balcon au bord de l'eau* (1949) p.249: Rouveyre at the Bas-Bréau, then the Charmettes.
- Francis Carco, André Billy, Rouveyre: portraits of the three hung in the Charmettes dining room (Aristide Marie, *La forêt symboliste*, p.249). *Vient de paraître* 1928 p.243: Rouveyre and Carco at the Charmettes.
- Champigneulle, *Promenades dans Fontainebleau* (1965) p.257: Rouveyre lived at the Charmettes; aperitif at the Charmettes bar, then boules "chez Marius".
- Paul Léautaud, letters to Marie Dormoy, 9 Sept 1939: telephoned the Hôtel des Charmettes looking for her (*Lettres à Marie Dormoy*, 1989, p.261). Auguste Perret wrote to Dormoy from the Charmettes on Salon des Tuileries letterhead (Perret-Dormoy correspondence 1922-1953, 2009, p.462). Léautaud *Journal littéraire* index: Charmettes (Les) pp.19, 20, 283.
- Mounet-Sully: a note sent from the Hôtel des Charmettes recorded in the Comédie-Française daily register (Penesco, *Mounet-Sully*, 2005, p.525; date cut off; before 1916). LEAD.
- Marcellin Berthelot anecdote: Schaller crying "Vite ! Une chaise pour M. Berthelot !" (Billy, *Les beaux jours de Barbizon* p.122; repeated Revue d'histoire de la pharmacie 1946 p.533). Before 1907. Billy secondhand. LEAD.
- Lloyd George at the Charmettes: only in Herbert Kranz, *Die "Sieger" nachher* (1939), Nazi-era satire. NOT usable.

### The hotel
- Billy, *Les beaux jours de Barbizon* p.110: Charmettes "fondées sept ou huit ans auparavant par un hôtelier suisse, Schaller, qui avait l'art difficile de plaire aux gens de lettres et de théâtre". Billy wrong on founder (Duhamel 1896); right on Schaller's reputation.
- Touring-Club de France, *À la France: sites et monuments* (1901-1903) p.46: Barbizon "avec l'hôtel des Charmettes, qui, comme tous les autres d'ailleurs, est le rendez-vous des touristes". Full view available ("Lire").
- Joanne, *Environs de Paris* (1903) p.246: four main hotels incl. the Charmettes, pension from 6 fr.
- *La Locomotion automobile* 1904 p.259: first outing of the "Audax" motorists, lunch at the Charmettes served by "M. Challer" [Schaller], champagne offered by the proprietor.
- *Touring* (Revue du Touring-Club) 1908 p.254: "notre camarade M. Schaller continue la transformation selon les principes du Touring-Club" -> the hotel was being rebuilt/modernised c.1908. KEY for AD77 constructions nouvelles.
- Association des chimistes de sucrerie 1908/1909 p.1113: excursion lunch at the Charmettes, 4 fr wine included.
- Newnham-Davis, *Gourmet's Guide to Europe* (1911) p.53: Charmettes "fairly comfortable and picturesque", meals under an awning in the garden in fine weather, crowded in summer.
- Société de géographie de Lille bulletin 1912 p.185: lunch under a beech tree at the Charmettes.
- Baedeker *Paris and its Environs* 1924 p.425: Hôtel des Charmettes, room from 12 fr, breakfast 3, lunch or dinner 20 fr.
- *Time and Tide* 1929: recommended, from 10s a day.
- Karampournis 1990 reproduces an advert "GRAND HOTEL DES CHARMETTES ... Maison SCHALLER".
- *Revue culinaire* 1936 p.76: chef M. Cazeau-Mesplé.
- 1933 *Le Monde hôtelier*: listed.
- 1973-1974: used as a seminar venue (Génie rural 1974; Gazette apicole 1974).
- *Paris en scène* (Dorian, 1956) p.274: anecdote of lunching alone at the Charmettes.
- Rouveyre settled for good at Barbizon in 1933 (Biographies des principales personnalités françaises, 1964, p.249) and died there 18 Dec 1962 (Apollinaire editions, note). Matisse corresponded with him there; Gérald Cramer visited him "dans son antre" (Ehrenström 1988 p.125).

## Other places: checks on published claims

### Villa Élisabeth (30 GR), Roland Dorgelès
- Billy, *Les beaux jours de Barbizon* (1947) p.160: Dorgelès, a regular of "le Nid", RENTED a villa on the Grande Rue, the Villa Elisabeth, where he wrote *Le Château des brouillards* (1932) and where his wife Hania sang. Champigneulle 1965 p.257: Dorgelès rented a villa for the summer opposite Millet's studio. -> Site says "ancienne résidence": OK; add "louée", *Le Château des brouillards*.
- Same passage (Champigneulle): after Dorgelès, Henri Matisse spent several months in the village. LEAD.

### Manoir Saint-Hérem (ex Hôtel Bellevue), Picasso
- No book places Picasso at Barbizon. Documented: summer 1921, Picasso, Olga and Paulo at Fontainebleau (L'Œil, April 2007, p.47). The Bellevue claim rests on the mairie map only. FLAG for Luigi (Tier 1 map vs no corroboration).

### Maison de Charles Jacque (24 GR)
- "Le Raphaël des moutons" is the period nickname of the Flemish painter Balthasar Ommeganck: *Le noir et le blanc, ou ma promenade au Salon* (1812) p.21; *L'étranger et l'anversois au salon d'Anvers* (1813) p.14; Marmottan, *L'École française de peinture* (1886) p.184. Applied to Jacque only in recent popular books (BTS manual 2025; Guide du Routard 2023). -> Remove from short_description (dossier already says not attested).

### Maison Diaz (28 GR)
- Lemoyne de Forges, *Barbizon et l'école de Barbizon* (1971) p.121: "Grande Rue et 2, route de Macherin. Une ancienne ferme s'élevait ici, à l'angle de la rue des Fermes, devenue route de Fleury. C'est là que séjourna Diaz de la Pena et, après lui, Hippolyte Taine qui écrivit, en 1867 ..."
- Exhibition catalogue *Barbizon au temps de J.-F. Millet 1849-1875* (1975) p.194: Diaz lived at Barbizon in an old farm at the corner of the Grande Rue and the route de Macherin.
- Leprohon, *L'Univers des peintres* (1985) p.74: [Diaz] bought an old farm at that corner, now the "Relais de Barbizon"; godfather of one of Millet's last sons.
- Moreau-Nélaton, *Millet raconté par lui-même* (1921) p.165: Diaz chosen by Millet as godfather of his son. Miquel, *Diaz* (2006) p.118: Diaz quoted "J'ai été le parrain de l'un d'eux"; 1861 Diaz stays at Barbizon and Chailly after his son's death.
- CONFLICT with site: site puts Maison Diaz at 28 Grande Rue and says he lived there "from 1837 until his death". Diaz died at Menton, 1876; his Barbizon presence was by stays. Secondary sources place his house at the Relais de Barbizon corner. FLAG for Luigi; check mairie map and plaque wording.

### Hôtellerie du Bas-Bréau (22 GR)
- Billy, *Les beaux jours de Barbizon* p.98: after 1870 Emmanuel Siron, former timber and carpentry merchant, turned [his house] into a hotel; the Hôtellerie du Bas-Bréau replaced the Hôtel Siron. Champigneulle p.254: Stevenson came to live at the Hôtel Siron in 1875. Debureaux 2022/2024: plaque says Stevenson had room no. 3.
- Site full_description (EN): "Established over 200 years ago as a hunting lodge" UNSUPPORTED. Replace with Siron's hotel after 1870, Stevenson 1875.

### Auberge Ganne
- Pomarède et al., *L'école de Barbizon: peindre en plein air* (Lyon, 2002) p.109: François Ganne (1797-1861), tailor, and his wife; in 1834 they bought a large house on the Grande Rue, the museum since 1995. Earlier grocery offering cheap board (Caille, in Grigorescu catalogue, Agen 2006, p.52). Daguenet 2002 p.132: inn opened around 1824 (the earlier business). -> opening of the inn in the present house: 1834.
- Ganne registers show Diaz-circle painters 1849-1861 (1975 catalogue p.300).
- Luniot: Edmond Luniot married Victoire Ganne; after the Franco-Prussian war the couple left the old inn and Luniot carried its painted decor to his new hotel (Lemoyne de Forges 1962 p.64: "L'Hôtel des Artistes dans lequel Luniot avait transporté le décor de l'auberge de son beau-père"; Grigorescu catalogue 2006 p.55; Müllerschön 2002 p.39: he let the old grocery shop, raised pension prices). Lyon 2002 p.109: "hôtel Luniot-Ganne qui sera détruit par la famille en 1959" (snippet, unclear whether hotel or decor). The name "hôtel Luniot-Ganne" already in Bellier de La Chavignerie 1868 p.764 (a watercolour of its courtyard), so the inn carried that name before the move.
- Painted panels from the Luniot heirs (former owners of the Auberge Ganne) acquired by the municipality on 9 April 1990 (Mantes-la-Jolie catalogue *Face à face*, 1998, p.116). Karampournis 1990 p.1931: many souvenirs of the Ganne inn were taken by Luniot and dispersed.

### Hôtel de la Forêt (site of bobo-club, 1 GR)
- Karampournis 1990 p.1897: the Bizon's Club occupies the site of the old Hôtel de la Forêt run by "le père Masson", who organised the banquet for the officials at the tramway inauguration.
- Billy, *Les beaux jours de Barbizon* p.124: Masson, owner of the Hôtel de la Forêt, gave the company the plot on which the little station was built; Masson had been a painter "d'un certain talent".
- Plancke, *Histoire du chemin de fer de Seine-et-Marne* (1991) p.135 and *Melun à la Belle Époque* (1992) p.259: official train arrived at noon, Orphéon aubade, lunch at the Hôtel de la Forêt (1899 inauguration).
- Darlington, *Newport, Cowes ...* (1898) p.40, advert: "Grand Hotel de la Forêt ... MASSON, PROPRIETOR. Telegraphic Address: 'Hotel-Foret, Barbizon.' Telephone No. 1." Keep wording "premier téléphone mis à la disposition du public".
- *La Nature* 1892 p.461: excursion lunch at the Hôtel de la Forêt "bien connu des artistes".
