## 1. Hunt, Le Semeur and the Boston fire of 1872

**Question.** The Besharat page said the canvases Hunt brought home,
« dont Le Semeur de Millet », burned in the 1872 fire. Luigi's long-held
understanding, from an earlier source, was that several Millets burned.

**Found.**
- *Le Semeur* did not burn. MFA Boston 17.1485: sold by Millet to Hunt
  c.1851-53, sold by Hunt to Quincy Adams Shaw in 1874, given to the MFA in
  1917. Tier 1. [5313ac5f, d14c5174 refuted]
- Hunt did lose Millets. Helen M. Knowlton, *Art-Life of William Morris
  Hunt* (1899), p. 184, verified in the archive.org OCR: « He had bought a
  dozen or more important works by Millet » ... « Five of his valuable
  Millets were burned when his studio was destroyed by the great fire in
  Summer Street in 1872. » The five hanging in his Beacon Street house
  escaped and were sold to Shaw for $10,000. Tier 2 (Hunt's pupil, writing
  27 years later). [82e508d6, lead, publishable with attribution]
- The five survivors are the five MFA Millet oils from Hunt via Shaw:
  17.1484, 17.1485 (*The Sower*), 17.1488, 17.1489, 17.1490. Tier 1.
- Hunt's studio in the Mercantile Building, Summer Street, burned (MFA
  69.1127, Hunt's drawing *The Boston Fire*). With it went many of his own
  works, his portrait of Lincoln (Knowlton p. 41) and the studies for
  *Anahita* (Knowlton p. 146). [13c35b15]
- Knowlton's fire chapter (pp. 143-144, read through search snippets only)
  mentions a closet of « choice paintings by Millet and Diaz », a Millet
  nymph lying on autumn grass and a study of a cow. These are probably among
  the burned works, but that link is our inference.
- Other versions of *Le Semeur*: National Museum Wales NMW A 2474 (1847-48),
  Yamanashi no. 284 (1850), Carnegie (1870, trade press only), pastels at
  the Clark (c.1865) and the Walters. None linked to Hunt. [8a783568]

**How the story grew.** Knowlton 1899 « five valuable Millets » >
Britannica 11th ed. vol. 13 (1910) « five large Millets and other art
treasures » (« large » appears here with nothing behind it) > Wikipedia and
genealogy pages (Wikipedia's footnote cites vol. 27, apparently wrong) > a
March 2026 drafting session on this project wrote « five major Millet
canvases ... all destroyed », with The Sower among them. Each copy added
something.

**Not found.** Any 1872 newspaper, letter or inventory naming Hunt's
Millet losses; any title of a burned Millet.

**Not read (blocked or unreachable from the session).** Angell, *Records of
William M. Hunt* (1881); Chronicling America, Nov-Dec 1872; Google Books;
Hoppin 1979; Murphy, MFA 1984; Webster 1991; page images of Knowlton
pp. 143-144 (the sentence joining the two pages). Good targets for the
morning local job (S4).

## 2. Galerie L'Angélus and the Millet museum

Luigi, 15 Oct: the Galerie L'Angélus is across the street from the Millet
museum, at 34 Grande Rue, and shows modern and contemporary art. It is not
a room of the museum. Same family runs the gallery, the Musée Millet
(27-29 GR) and Nunchi (36 bis GR). Noted on all three locations'
`internal_notes`. The Office de Tourisme listings that place the gallery at
27 or 36 bis mix the family's places up.

## 3. Musée Millet opening year

**Found.** 1923, not 1922. The museum's own site: « Ouvert depuis 1923 »;
Visit Paris Region: « Transformé en musée privé en 1923 ». Our page said
1922; corrected in French and English. [909949e8]
Winter hours (closed Tuesday and Wednesday, November to March) and prices
(5 €, children 4 €) from Visit Paris Region.

## 4. Auberge Ganne

**Found.** Commune purchase 1987, museum open 1995, Conseil général of
Seine-et-Marne from 2004 (POP / Muséofile M0370, Tier 1). The function box
said « municipal museum since 1993 »: wrong. Also on the notice and not yet
on the site: a first municipal museum in Rousseau's studio in 1927 (closed
c.1930) and a smaller one there in 1977, accredited 1981. [32797e83]

## 5. Parcours des mosaïques

**Found.** Twenty mosaics in « verre de Venise » (Visit Paris Region,
Cirkwi); mosaicists Arnaud Péreira, Cécile Barbara, Nathalie Grangé; trail
inaugurated 17 April 2010 (Cirkwi). Tier 3, to confirm with the mairie.
The route text « ceramic mosaics » was wrong. [7cb77f8b]

**Not found.** Any source naming Murano. Venetian mosaic glass (smalti) is
made in Venice and on Murano; the supplier of these mosaics is not named,
so the site says « verre de Venise ». Also not verified: that each mosaic
stands where its painting was made (removed from the tour text); Cirkwi
lists 19 entries for the 20 announced.
